# Website brief and journeys

Describe what the website must help a real visitor understand, feel, trust, and do. A list such as hero, features, testimonials, CTA is not a page specification.

## Establish evidence and scope

Read relevant project material, available research or analytics, existing content and assets, constraints, and approved decisions. Note which sources were actually inspected. Distinguish confirmed facts, assumptions and open decisions with source pointers. Existing approved audience or sitemap decisions should not be reopened without a relevant conflict.

Ask related questions together when they change the site: who arrives, from where, with what need or doubt; what business outcome matters; what evidence and assets exist; where the desired action leads. Offer labeled assumptions for reversible choices when speed matters. Missing case-study evidence is a content gap, not permission to invent results.

## Work from the visitor decision

1. Separate the business goal from the website's contribution. Name an observable success signal; mark targets as proposed unless supplied. Do not fabricate analytics baselines or imply that tracking has been installed.
2. Identify primary and secondary audiences by needs, context, and objections. Avoid unsupported demographic personas.
3. Trace important journeys from entry point through questions, evidence, decisions, desired action, and completion. Include direct entry to a case study, article or service page when relevant; not every journey starts at the homepage.
4. Derive the sitemap and navigation from those journeys. State why each page exists and how the visitor moves onward. For a one-page site, use meaningful anchors and justify why a single page suffices.
5. Decide which claims need proof, which assets communicate identity, and how calls to action match visitor readiness. Include the destination and completion experience for contact, purchase, download or other actions.

Use [brief contracts](references/brief-contracts.md) for journey, page and proof entries. Keep strategy, page behavior, and technical acceptance in separate sections of an existing brief. Split substantial page briefs only when necessary; do not force a document per page or a page per section.

## Specify pages proportionally

For every distinct page or shared page template, record audience/context, purpose and primary action, key/supporting messages, proof and content, the justified sequence, interactions, mobile behavior, and observable acceptance criteria. Each sequence entry answers a visitor question and explains why it belongs there. State content priority and reading order before choosing a grid.

Name relevant empty, loading, error and success states, keyboard behavior, media and motion requirements. Mark absent features not applicable instead of inventing forms, modals or filters. Link exact visual treatment to `DESIGN.md`; do not put a second token system in the brief.

## Check and hand back

Walk each priority journey using only the documents. Can a visitor find the evidence and take the action? Can a builder distinguish verified copy from proposed copy and identify unavailable assets? Does each required page have a purpose beyond completing a template?

Return the compact brief, material open decisions, and readiness for visual direction or specification review. A complete brief does not approve a visual identity or authorize site implementation.
